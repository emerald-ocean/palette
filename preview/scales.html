How the tests work

Contrast (APCA Lc). Measures how readable text is against its background, based on how the eye actually perceives lightness. It replaces the older WCAG ratio, which overrates dark colours on dark backgrounds. Targets come from the APCA guidelines:

  • Lc 60+: code text, the minimum for comfortable reading of normal-weight text.
  • Lc 45+: comments and punctuation, readable but meant to recede.
  • Lc 30+: line numbers and other non-text UI, visible but not read continuously.
  • Lc 85–92 for strings: very bright text on a near-black background glows ("halation"), which tires the eyes, so the brightest text is capped.

Each colour is also checked on the selection background, so text stays readable when selected.

Colour blindness (simulation). Each colour is converted to how it looks with protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, using the Machado et al. (2009) model at full severity, the worst case. About 8% of men and 0.5% of women have red-green colour blindness (protan or deutan); tritanopia is rare. Red-green colour-blind eyes still see lightness and blue versus yellow, so that is how neighbouring colours are kept apart.

Neighbour separation (OKLab ΔE). The perceptual distance between colours of tokens that often sit next to each other, such as store.Next(. A difference of about 0.02 is just noticeable on a large patch; words are small, so they need much more to separate at a glance. The thresholds are our own, set by eye in the preview:

  • ≥ 0.10 for normal vision: clearly distinct at a glance.
  • ≥ 0.07 under colour blindness: distinguishable, and the best the optimiser could reach while keeping every colour within its contrast range.

These tests catch problems; they cannot prove comfort. The final check is your eyes after a day of use.
